# Barbs

A dimension is a group element, and [Dimensions](./dimensions.md) shows the group.
What that page cannot say is *what makes two dimensions equal in a way an observer
can see*. A group with a decidable equality is one thing; an account of equality
that a context can test is another, and the second is what makes this a calculus
rather than a bookkeeping convention.

## A barb

A **barb** is an action a process can perform immediately. Write `P ↓ a` for *`P`
has a barb at `a`*: no reduction is needed, `a` is on offer now.

The barb set here is indexed by three things:

```
P ↓ (c, f, δ)
```

— the process offers, on channel `c`, the field `f` at dimension `δ`. A channel is
a name; a field is a component of the record a channel carries; a dimension is one
of the group elements above.

## What a barb does not see, and why that is the definition rather than a shortcut

**A barb records the dimension of what a channel carries and not its magnitude.**
That is the load-bearing decision on this page, and taking it the other way is
what makes the account collapse.

If a barb could see the magnitude, then barbed congruence would separate any two
quantities with different numbers, and dimension would not characterise it — the
whole correspondence below would be false. The reason to restrict the barb set is
not that the restriction is convenient. It is that **the interface of a channel is
its type**: a context that reads a number off a channel and does arithmetic with it
is not observing the channel, it is computing with what the channel delivered. An
observer of an interface sees the interface.

So the fragment this page is about is the *dimension-respecting* one — contexts
built from the algebra (product, inverse, integer and rational powers) and from
sending and receiving on typed channels, with no other operation. That fragment
is where a dimension is observable, and it is exactly the fragment a unit
operation's arithmetic lives in. The full language, magnitude and all, is
[Processes](./process.md); dimensional equality is the *type-level* part of its
observation, and this page is the statement of which part that is.

## Bisimulation, and congruence

The definitions are the standard ones, and they are stated rather than assumed
because the claim at the end is about them.

- **Barbed bisimulation.** A symmetric relation `R` is a barbed bisimulation when
  `P R Q` implies: for every `P → P'` there is `Q ⇒ Q'` with `P' R Q'`, and
  `P ↓ a` iff `Q ↓ a`. Write `P ≈̇ Q` for the largest such relation.
- **Barbed congruence.** `P ≅ Q` when `C[P] ≈̇ C[Q]` for every context `C` in the
  dimension-respecting fragment. This is the largest congruence contained in
  barbed bisimilarity, and it is the right notion of "indistinguishable" because it
  quantifies over every way a caller can use the thing.

Barbed bisimilarity alone is not a congruence — a context can put a process where
its barbs are hidden — which is why the definition above closes under contexts
rather than stopping at `≈̇`.

## The claims

**Claim (dimension equality is barbed congruence).** For quantities `Q` and `Q'`:

```
dim Q = dim Q'   ⟺   no dimension-respecting context distinguishes them
```

*Status: **specified**. There is no barb layer in this repository, no channel type
and no process calculus, so nothing here could falsify this claim either way.
`Azoth.Barb.dimensional_eq_iff_barbed_congruent` is what the tranche that builds
the barb layer is checked against, and it is proved then - see
[the status table](./index.md).*

The two directions are not symmetric in difficulty. `⇐` is a congruence argument:
if the dimensions agree, no barb can differ, because barbs observe only the
dimension and every context is built from operations the dimension map respects.
`⇒` is the interesting one: given two *different* dimensions, exhibit the context
that separates them. The separating context is the one that puts both quantities on
the same channel field — the same `(c, f)` — which is exactly the operation a
unit operation performs and the operation dimensional analysis exists to forbid.

**Claim (a composite dimension is observable through its parts).** The barb set is
generated by the algebra: `P ↓ (c, f, δ + ε)` is derivable from `P ↓ (c, f, δ)` and
`P ↓ (c, f, ε)` by the product rule, and `P ↓ (c, f, n·δ)` from `n` copies by the
power rule. So the barbs of a channel at a derived dimension are determined by its
barbs at the generators, and **checking a derived dimension never requires checking
more than the seven exponents**.

*Status: **specified**, for the same reason and with the same consequence:
`Azoth.Barb.barbs_are_generated_by_the_algebra` is written when there is a barb
layer for it to be about.*

That second claim is the one that makes the first usable. Without it, "no context
distinguishes them" would be a judgement over an infinite space of contexts; with
it, the space collapses onto the seven slots, and the decision procedure is the one
the table already performs.

## Why this page exists rather than a paragraph on the previous one

Because the two pages answer different questions and only one of them is algebraic.
[Dimensions](./dimensions.md) says what the group is and that its equality is
decidable. This page says *what equality of dimension means to something using it*
— and that turns out to be exactly the observable content of every well-typed
context, which is the property that lets the type layer stand in for a runtime
check without weakening anything.

The counterpart in the other direction is [The keycard](./capability.md), whose two
claims are also observational: authority is what a process can *do*, and
non-amplification is a statement about which barbs a result can have.
